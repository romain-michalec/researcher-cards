# 10 · Mapping, SLAM & AI Vision

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 10 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 10 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. There is no perception topic in Horizontal Activities, but two matched subjects — marine observation and environmental monitoring — carry real perception work at project scale.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under Computer Vision and Perception with a 2026-2027 window and a value of 5-10 million euro. There is no computer vision or perception topic in Horizontal Activities as such. The horizontal instruments that fund perception and vision work are concentrated in three places: the ocean observation topic HORIZON-NATURE-2027-04-01, where sensor technology, observing system experiments and data assimilation are the substance; the autonomous mobility topic, where perception in complex urban scenarios and infrastructure to augment vehicle perception are explicit; and the biodiversity monitoring topic, which asks for automated and real-time monitoring supported by AI where possible, and whose call introduction gives AI identification from images and sounds as an example.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 5-10 million euro. The relevant horizontal values are 50.00 million euro for the single ocean observation project, 15.00 million euro for the single mobility project, and 16.00 million euro across three biodiversity monitoring projects at 5.00 to 6.00 million euro each. The biodiversity monitoring band matches the deck's range most closely at project level.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows 2026-2027. The relevant deadlines are 16 February 2027 for the ocean observation and climate topics, 14 January 2027 for the nature-positive 01 and 02 call groups, 16 September 2027 for the biodiversity and health topic, and 18 March 2027 for the mobility topic.

## Scope as drafted

The biodiversity monitoring topic, HORIZON-NATURE-2027-02-05, is the closest project-scale match and is worth setting out because it is explicitly an AI and sensing topic in substance. It requires more precise and more efficient biodiversity monitoring schemes building on advanced technologies and more abundant data, and more efficient and user-friendly tools for policymakers and competent authorities to implement and evaluate EU environmental legislation. Proposals must address one of three areas and identify it: Area A, the Nature Restoration Regulation, namely the EU Pollinators Monitoring Scheme; Area B, soil biodiversity for the Soil Monitoring Law; or Area C, the Water Framework Directive.

Across all three areas, proposals must develop scalable approaches to improve the cost-effectiveness of legally binding monitoring schemes, integrating different technologies and methods and engaging end-users; develop demonstration cases to test, operationalise and pilot monitoring approaches, including where possible automated and real-time monitoring frameworks supported by AI; assess and compare approaches for scalability, gaps, limitations and drawbacks; build on identified methods and technologies for species identification and quantification, including DNA approaches; and advance the use of genomic techniques in monitoring species abundance for integration into indicator-based frameworks. Proposals must also complete or substantially advance DNA libraries for the relevant groups in each area.

The ocean observation topic, HORIZON-NATURE-2027-04-01, is the largest perception-adjacent instrument in Horizontal Activities. Aligned with the OceanEye initiative, it aims to strengthen and stabilise the Global Ocean Observing System, reinforce governance frameworks and networks operating under the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission, and integrate European biodiversity and ecosystem observing efforts into the global landscape. It requires observing system experiments, observing system simulation experiments or network optimisation approaches to design a reliable, sustained, cost-effective global observing system covering coverage, resolution, accuracy and data redundancy, strengthening the link between in situ and remote sensing monitoring programmes and optimising data assimilation and modelling. It requires identification of critical technology needs including AI and co-creation with industry of a multiplatform technology roadmap including environmental, biogeochemical and biological sensors, demonstration of high-TRL sensors measuring Essential Ocean Variables with enhanced data integration and interoperability, and skills development, training and knowledge exchange across regions.

The mobility topic's perception content is that data and AI model sharing must define requirements for interoperable digital infrastructures and federated data-sharing and AI training frameworks, including the role of infrastructure in augmenting vehicle perception in complex urban scenarios, safeguarding industrial participants' interests and enabling safe and cybersecure-by-design operation.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- More precise and more efficient biodiversity monitoring schemes building on advanced technologies and more abundant data.
- More efficient and user-friendly tools for policymakers and competent authorities, leading to simplified policy implementation and better protection and restoration of ecosystems.
- For ocean observation: strengthened and resilient global ocean observing capacities coordinated under GOOS; public authorities prioritising investment in infrastructure and capacity building; increased EU leadership and strategic autonomy in global ocean observation; and enhanced collaboration between observation communities, marine research infrastructures, SMEs, technology providers and maritime industries.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Biodiversity monitoring: Research and Innovation Action, 16.00 million euro across three projects of 5.00 to 6.00 million euro; balanced-portfolio procedure guaranteeing the highest-ranked proposal in each of areas A, B and C; lump-sum financing; use of Copernicus or Galileo and EGNOS if satellite data are used.
- Ocean observation: Innovation Action, 50.00 million euro for a single project, page limit 100 pages; all international organisations exceptionally eligible for funding; Chinese-controlled entities barred; in-situ and marine observation data must be made openly available through EMODnet on FAIR principles; the evaluation committee will be composed partly of representatives of EU institutions; equipment and infrastructure purchased specifically for the action may be declared as full capitalised costs; the topic forms part of a coordination initiative between the European Space Agency and the Commission on Earth System Science under the Ocean Flagship.
- Mobility: Innovation Action, TRL 8, 15.00 million euro for one project.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This area is one of the strongest fits across the 16 areas, because the National Robotarium's marine, perception and sensing capability maps directly onto the ocean observation topic and its autonomy capability maps onto the mobility topic.

The listed companies that fit best are the marine group — Frontier Robotics, HonuWorx, SeeByte, Coda Octopus and Flowcopter — plus Cyberhawk and Novosound on sensing and inspection. The ocean observation topic is a single very large project and will be led by a major consortium; the realistic route is as a perception and autonomy partner with a defined work package, and the biodiversity monitoring topic is a more attainable lead.

## Points to watch

- There is no dedicated perception or SLAM topic. The two matched subjects are marine observation and environmental monitoring, so the proposal must be framed in those terms.
- The ocean observation topic is a single-project competition of 50 million euro with an evaluation committee partly composed of EU institution representatives; consortium politics will matter as much as the technology.
- The biodiversity monitoring topic requires DNA library work alongside the AI and sensing work, which is a substantial additional commitment and needs a genomics partner.
