# 12 · Industrial Inspection Robotics

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 12 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 12 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. The value band matches the Clean Industrial Deal topics exactly, but neither is an inspection robotics topic in name, and the 2026 vintage has already closed.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under the Clean Industrial Deal with a window of late 2026 to 2027 and a value of 10-25 million euro. Two topics in the Clean Industrial Deal call carry decarbonisation-of-industry content in the Work Programme, and the deck's value band is close to theirs, but neither is an inspection robotics topic in name.

- HORIZON-CID-2026-01-01: R&I in Support of the Clean Industrial Deal: Decarbonisation of energy intensive industries (Innovation Action), implemented through the Processes4Planet and Clean Steel partnerships.
- HORIZON-CID-2027-01-01: the 2027 equivalent, again an Innovation Action under Processes4Planet and Clean Steel.
- HORIZON-CID-2026-01-02 and HORIZON-CID-2027-01-02: Clean Technologies for Climate Action, the companion open topics.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 10-25 million euro. The Work Programme band starts higher: both energy-intensive-industry topics carry a total indicative budget of 125.00 million euro with an expected EU contribution of between 15.00 and 25.00 million euro per project and an indicative eight projects. The 2026 and 2027 Clean Technologies for Climate Action topics carry 150.00 million euro and 140.00 million euro respectively, again at 15.00 to 25.00 million euro per project across eight projects. Call totals are 275.00 million euro for 2026 and 265.00 million euro for 2027.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows late 2026 to 2027. The Clean Industrial Deal 2026 call opened on 18 December 2025 with a deadline of 15 September 2026, so that vintage has already closed. The 2027 call opens on 12 January 2027 with a deadline of 15 September 2027, and that is the live opportunity.

## Scope as drafted

The call's stated purpose is to support the development of a new generation of demonstrators in clean tech and decarbonised industry that combine technological excellence with market readiness, to accelerate market deployment. It follows a bottom-up, industry-led approach, allowing cross-sectoral system integration and a market-driven response to industry needs, and consists of two large open topics: clean tech for climate, and decarbonisation of energy-intensive industries. A complementary 50 million euro call in support of the Clean Industrial Deal is included in the Research Fund for Coal and Steel. Expected outputs are projects that are, in the Work Programme's phrase, fit for deployment: technically feasible, with a realistic business plan and a sound market-readiness strategy.

The energy-intensive-industry topic requires proposals to contribute to all three of the following outcomes: accelerating the use of innovative technologies to decarbonise industrial processes and bring more cost-effective clean products to market; creating new first-of-a-kind operational demonstrators or optimising newly installed industrial decarbonisation solutions in Europe; and demonstrating market readiness of the future clean products and processes through a credible business plan and an industrialisation exploitation strategy including market-tested use cases.

The scope covers three technology areas. Area one is managing the carbon cycle through carbon capture and utilisation or storage, with optimisation and demonstration of solutions for capture, utilisation or storage of carbon dioxide or carbon monoxide from energy-intensive installations, targeting a 30 per cent reduction in energy input per tonne relative to current technology, with commercialisation potential assessed on lifecycle analysis, market size and cost. Area two is clean energy usage in production, covering electrification of processes, decarbonated production, integration of alternative clean energy carriers such as hydrogen, on-site renewable energy storage and use and upgrading of waste heat, supporting major improvements in clean energy use in energy-intensive industries until 2035. Area three is circularity and resource efficiency of production processes in material, energy and water, targeting a 30 per cent improvement by 2035 with commercially viable technological solutions and significant reductions in raw material consumption, energy input, freshwater intake, ecosystem impact and emissions, through circular value networks that convert industrial side-streams and end-of-use waste into new feedstock, with an overall positive lifecycle analysis.

Proposals must explicitly select one main area but may address a combination in an integrated way within an industrial sector. They must demonstrate adequate integration of relevant technologies, directly or indirectly, and show industrial leadership in deployment after the project, with the consortium industry-driven, composed of a preferably small and manageable number of participants with a justified size; SME participation is encouraged. The dissemination, exploitation and communication plan must include a sound business plan and market-readiness strategy covering the targeted market, estimated total addressable market size in the EU and globally, the competitive landscape, financial projections including revenue, profitability, investment and cash flow, user and customer needs, and the expected market position and competitive advantage. The market-readiness strategy must identify obstacles and necessary actions — investment commitment for the next phase including possible synergies with other programmes, regulatory constraints, permitting conditions, standards, skills, product robustness, industrial integrators, intellectual property and user acceptance — and must include a comprehensive risk assessment. Proposals must include a clear go/no-go moment ahead of the contracting and demonstration phase, before which the project must deliver detailed engineering plans, a techno-economic assessment, all needed permits for the demonstrator, a complete business plan and market-readiness strategy identifying the industrial partners who will lead deployment.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Accelerated roll-out and deployment of European decarbonised and clean tech solutions across EU industrial sectors such as decarbonised industries, energy and transport.
- Support for the European competitiveness of the next generation of clean tech and decarbonised industry.
- Resilience and strategic autonomy through expanded European manufacturing capacity for industry decarbonisation and clean tech solutions, and strengthened sustainable and resilient value chains reducing dependencies.
- Mobilisation and alignment of public and private financing and investment for innovative clean energy and industry decarbonisation technologies, assets, grids and processes in the EU.
- A reduction in the energy price gap in Europe through deployment of competitive clean tech and industrial decarbonisation solutions.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Innovation Action.
- Technology readiness: activities are expected to start at TRL 6 and achieve TRL 7 to 8 by the end of the project for energy-intensive industries, and TRL 8 by the end for clean technologies for climate action.
- Page limit: exceptionally extended to a maximum of 60 pages in part B, to accommodate the business plan and market-readiness strategy.
- Procedure: to contribute to a balanced portfolio across the three technology areas, grants are awarded not only in order of ranking but at least to one application that is the highest ranked for each area, provided all thresholds are met.
- Legal and financial set-up: the granting authority may, up to four years after the end of the action, object to a transfer of ownership or to the exclusive licensing of results.
- 2027 vintage only: in line with the restriction on control in innovation actions in critical technology areas, entities established in an eligible country but directly or indirectly controlled by China or by a legal entity established in China are not eligible to participate.
- Satellite data use requires Copernicus or Galileo and EGNOS.
- Consortium size in the clean technology topic is indicatively not more than ten participants.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This area is best read as the industrial robotics entry point into the Clean Industrial Deal, and the honest position is that the topics as drafted are decarbonisation and clean technology topics where robotics is a means rather than the subject. The plausible robotics framings are autonomous inspection and maintenance of industrial assets, which speaks to the circularity and resource efficiency area and to asset integrity in energy-intensive plants; robotic solutions for handling and processing industrial side-streams; and sensing and monitoring for emissions and process control. That maps well onto the National Robotarium's inspection and autonomous systems capability and onto the listed Industrial and Environmental Systems companies — Cyberhawk on aerial inspection, Novosound on ultrasonic sensing, RoboK on perception, Reath on circular economy tracking, Reactec on industrial exposure monitoring, Utopi on building monitoring and Singul8 on automation.

The academic leads would sit with the robotics and systems groups at Heriot-Watt and the University of Edinburgh. Because the topic is industry-driven and expects the consortium to be small and manageable with a credible business plan, the lead partner should be an industrial company, with the universities supplying the demonstrator technology. The Delivery Board should treat the 2026 vintage as closed and plan for the January 2027 opening.

## Points to watch

- The 2026 Clean Industrial Deal call closed on 15 September 2026, so this area can only be pursued through the 2027 call, opening 12 January 2027 and closing 15 September 2027.
- Neither topic is an inspection robotics topic in name. A robotics bid has to be positioned inside decarbonisation, clean energy use or circularity and resource efficiency.
- The 60-page limit, the mandatory business plan and market-readiness strategy, the go/no-go gate and the requirement for a fully permitted demonstrator raise the bid cost substantially above a conventional research action.
- The 2027 topics carry the critical-technology control restriction, so partner screening for Chinese control is required early.
