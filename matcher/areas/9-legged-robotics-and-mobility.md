# 9 · Legged Robotics & Mobility

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 9 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 9 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. The call family named in the deck does not exist in Horizontal Activities, and there is no legged robotics topic in the source.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under a call family described as Advanced Locomotion, with a 2027 window and a value of 4-8 million euro. That call family does not exist in Horizontal Activities. There is no legged robotics topic in the horizontal part. The horizontal topics whose value band overlaps the deck's range are HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-02-02 at 2.50 to 4.00 million euro per project and HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-06 at around 5.00 million euro per project. The nearest subject match, although it is not a legged robotics topic, is the physical AI content referenced in the Frontier AI scaling topic, which names physical AI as one of the strategic sectors that scaled models should serve, and the autonomous systems content in the mobility and ocean topics.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 4-8 million euro. Within Horizontal Activities, the topics with per-project contributions in or near that band are HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-06 at around 5.00 million euro per project and HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-02-02 at 2.50 to 4.00 million euro per project. No horizontal topic funds legged robotics as a subject.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows 2027. The nearest dates are 28 January 2027 for the 02 call and 15 June 2027 for the 03 call.

## Scope as drafted

There is no legged robotics or legged locomotion scope in Horizontal Activities. The two legitimate framings available in the source are as follows. The first is physical AI inside the Frontier AI scaling topic, where proposals are expected to describe how scaling methodologies would benefit models adapted to the specific requirements of particular strategic sectors, physical AI being one of the four named. The second is autonomous operations in the mobility topic, where AI-enabled electric autonomous vehicles operating in real urban traffic are the demonstrator. Neither is a legged locomotion call, and the Delivery Board should treat this area as a subject that currently has no matched horizontal vehicle.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

The transferable outcomes from the adjacent topics are, from the scaling topic, robust predictive frameworks for model performance before large-scale compute investment and methodology that benefits physical AI systems; and from the mobility topic, demonstrated scalable autonomous operation in real environments with validated safety, cybersecurity and interoperability. A legged robotics bid framed through either would need to make the platform the vehicle for the stated outcome rather than the subject of the proposal.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- No dedicated topic, so the conditions that would apply are those of whichever adjacent topic is used: TRL 4-5 for the scaling topic, TRL 8 for the mobility topic, and the Chinese-entity participation bar that applies across the AI topics in the Bridging actions call.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

Legged locomotion is one of the areas where the Edinburgh partnership is genuinely world-leading. Flowcopter is the closest listed company, on novel rotorcraft rather than legs, and is the strongest mobility-adjacent industrial partner available.

## Points to watch

- No horizontal call topic matches this area. The four to eight million euro band is not evidenced as a legged robotics opportunity in the source.
- The realistic route is the mainstream Cluster 4 work programme or a robotics framing inside the Cluster 5 mobility content that the horizontal bridging topics reference.
- If pursued through the scaling topic, the proposal would be a methodology bid with physical AI as an application, which is a different consortium from a locomotion bid.
