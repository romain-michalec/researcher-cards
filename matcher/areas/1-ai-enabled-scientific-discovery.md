# 1 · AI-enabled Scientific Discovery

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 1 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 1 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. It is the strongest single overlap across the 16 areas between the Delivery Board's themes and a horizontal call, because laboratory automation is applied robotics.

## Work Programme call and topic

Call: AI in Science (HORIZON-RAISE-2026-01 and HORIZON-RAISE-2027-01). Topic: HORIZON-RAISE-2027-01-01, Automated Scientific Discovery (RAISE pilot), Research and Innovation Action. A second, food-focused mirror topic runs in parallel as HORIZON-RAISE-2027-01-02.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 8-15 million euro. The Work Programme funds it differently: the Automated Scientific Discovery topic carries a total indicative budget of 26.50 million euro with an expected EU contribution of around 9.00 million euro per project across an indicative three projects. Its food-focused mirror, HORIZON-RAISE-2027-01-02, carries 3.00 million euro for a single project of around 3.00 million euro. The combined 2027 AI in Science call is therefore 29.50 million euro, and the largest single grant available is around 9.00 million euro rather than 15 million.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows Q3-Q4 2026 consortium building with bids in Q1-Q2 2027. The Work Programme call overview gives an opening date of 13 October 2026 and a deadline of 2 February 2027. Consortium building therefore has to be complete before the end of 2026, and the bid-writing window is effectively November to January.

## Scope as drafted

The topic addresses the development of safe and trustworthy closed-loop scientific experimentation systems, by integrating laboratory automation with AI-driven decision-making and robust data infrastructures. Funded projects help scientific laboratories that already have an advanced level of automation and digitalisation to design, develop and test the intelligence layer that lets scientific instrumentation semi- or fully autonomously plan, run and analyse experiments — ideally in coordination with other laboratories, and without requiring a complete redesign of existing laboratory outfitting.

Proposals must incorporate comprehensive data management systems covering collection, storage, processing and sharing of experimental data: scalable and secure storage, efficient processing and analysis tools, and mechanisms for data sharing and collaboration across laboratories while ensuring security and privacy. Systems may incorporate AI-driven resource optimisation modules that minimise energy, reagent and material consumption across automated experimentation cycles, and must carry appropriate security and robustness by design. Proposals must demonstrate how an existing laboratory can be retrofitted with AI-driven systems to plan, execute and analyse experiments in a closed loop, incorporating human oversight and interaction to ensure accuracy, safety and ethical compliance.

The suggested research targets, which are non-exhaustive, include: autonomous or semi-autonomous adaptive AI systems, including agentic AI, that connect with laboratory instruments and robotics and can plan, act, learn and adapt within a validated safe pipeline; assistive and interactive safe AI-managed robotic systems that automate diverse experiments across varied hardware setups; scalable automation and networked AI systems enabling collaborative experimentation across multiple laboratories in different geographic locations and simultaneous execution of large volumes of experiments; real-time data processing and analytics enabling immediate feedback and dynamic adjustment during experiments; standards and protocols for interoperability between instruments, robotics and AI systems; intuitive user interfaces for human-machine interaction; AI-driven predictive maintenance to optimise equipment uptime and resource use; and exploration of how scientific automation technologies can be adapted for disciplines beyond the scope of the call.

The initial focus put forward is materials science, with impact areas including drug discovery, battery technologies, photovoltaics, carbon capture and storage, water purification, soil remediation, environmentally friendly fertilisers, alternative protein sources, and sustainable fabrics and dyes. Proposals should demonstrate close interdisciplinary collaboration between computer and AI scientists and domain scientists. The scope prioritises software development but does not exclude justified purchase of complementary equipment. International collaboration is encouraged.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Closed-loop scientific experimentation systems that integrate automation with AI-driven trustworthy decision-making in existing laboratory environments.
- Accelerated scientific discovery with increased efficiency and reproducibility.
- Improved scientific productivity.
- Advancement of laboratory automation, including best practice, challenges and opportunities for accelerating research and development.
- Prototype functional demonstrators that showcase the integration of automation with AI-driven decision-making.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Research and Innovation Action; the topic is a pilot under the RAISE initiative (Resource for AI Science in Europe).
- Legal entities established in China are not eligible to participate in any action falling under AI in Science — this is a hard eligibility bar for the whole call family.
- Proposals are expected to develop synergies with running Horizon Europe projects in the same field, notably those funded under HORIZON-CL4-INDUSTRY-2025-01-DIGITAL-61 (AI Foundation models in science, GenAI4EU).
- The wider AI in Science pilot package totals 100 million euro across five topics, drawn from Clusters 4 and 6, and may be increased by contributions from foundations or philanthropic actors as assigned revenue.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This is the strongest single overlap across the 16 areas between the deck's themes and a horizontal call, because laboratory automation is applied robotics. The natural academic pairing is computer and AI science with an experimental domain, so the relevant Heriot-Watt expertise spans the machine learning, robotics and reasoning groups, with the University of Edinburgh's robot-learning and reasoning strengths as natural partners on the intelligence layer.

On the industrial side, National Robotarium companies working on instrumentation, sensing and automation are the obvious route into a demonstrator — Atomic Architect (Brian Gerardot), Kelvin Nanotechnology and Neuranics for the sensor and instrumentation layer, and RoboK or Reath for perception and autonomy in experimental environments. A bid should be built around a concrete existing laboratory on the Heriot-Watt campus or at the National Robotarium that can be retrofitted, rather than a generic automation platform.

## Points to watch

- The deck's value band of 8-15 million euro corresponds to the whole call, not to a single grant: budget for an expected contribution of around 9 million euro.
- This is a single-topic competition with an indicative three projects funded, so consortium quality matters more than breadth of partners.
- The Chinese-entity restriction applies to every AI in Science action, which will constrain partner screening early.
