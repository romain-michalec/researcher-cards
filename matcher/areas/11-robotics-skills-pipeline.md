# 11 · Robotics Skills Pipeline

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 11 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 11 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. This area maps onto a funded topic, but the per-project value is roughly an order of magnitude below the deck's band — it is a company-led instrument, not a university bid.

## Work Programme call and topic

Call: Bridging actions 2027 (HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-05). Topic: HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-05-01, Skills Bridging Action, a Coordination and Support Action.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 2-5 million euro. The Work Programme allocates 30.00 million euro to the topic, but the expected EU contribution per project is between 0.15 and 0.30 million euro, with an indicative 130 projects funded. The deck's range is therefore far above the actual per-project value: this is a small-grant, high-volume instrument, and the 30 million euro is the pool rather than a project size. Indeed the deck's lower bound is roughly seven times the maximum individual grant.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows 2027. The topic opens on 20 October 2026 with a deadline of 16 February 2027.

## Scope as drafted

The action is designed to reduce labour and skills shortages in innovative SMEs through a transnational mobility mechanism that lets them recruit and train highly skilled research associates, to improve SME innovation capacity by supporting the ideation phase of research and innovation projects, and to give researchers new knowledge and skills in industrial innovation and business management through employment and training in innovative companies.

Its rationale is that Europe faces a growing challenge in meeting demand for skilled talent in strategic sectors including clean and circular technologies, energy, health, biotech, agriculture, bioeconomy, digital technologies, aerospace and civil security; that the progressive loss of women in STEM careers, described as the leaky pipeline, worsens these shortages; that nearly four in five SMEs report they cannot find the talent they need; and that lack of access to skills is a major barrier to SME innovation, especially among startups and scaleups. The action is not limited to STEM and is open to research associates and SME staff with a social sciences and humanities background who could bring social innovation practices or an interdisciplinary perspective.

It complements the Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions by providing additional work opportunities for researchers and early-career professionals holding a PhD, including but not limited to former MSCA fellows, who want private-sector experience, and it aims to capitalise on European Universities alliances by giving SMEs access to their training, knowledge, resources, networks and facilities. In line with the Choose Europe for Science initiative, it provides an EU lump sum grant to innovative SMEs that have struggled to recruit relevant talent and can demonstrate the skills cannot be found nationally; it builds on the Horizon 2020 European SME innovation associate pilot. To apply, SMEs must describe the skill shortages they face, with national context data where available, and the innovation idea for which they wish to recruit, with the task description and required knowledge and skills set out as a vacancy notice.

The grant lets an SME employ a highly skilled researcher for a minimum of 12 and a maximum of 24 months. During that period the research associate benefits from a training and development programme on industrial innovation and business management, delivered in-house or by an external provider such as a European Universities alliance. The grant covers a competitive salary; relocation costs including removal, travel for the researcher and immediate family, visa costs, translation of certificates, a temporary transition allowance, return relocation after the project, and family and childcare allowances; implementation of the training and development plan; and travel to conferences, workshops, technological and research infrastructures or other activities forming part of that plan. Any cost not directly related to the employment and training of the research associate, such as the actual implementation of the innovation project, is out of scope. Proposals are encouraged to envisage synergies with projects funded through the EIC Accelerator.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Innovative SMEs reduce labour and skill shortages through a transnational mobility mechanism that lets them recruit and train highly skilled research associates.
- SMEs improve innovation capacity to support the ideation phase of R&I projects, with increased participation of highly skilled staff through recruitment and training.
- Researchers gain new knowledge and skills in industrial innovation and business management through employment and training in innovative companies.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Coordination and Support Action; page limit of the application is 10 pages.
- Eligibility: the only entities eligible to apply as a mono-beneficiary are individual small and medium enterprises located in an EU Member State or associated country. This is a hard structural constraint.
- Financing: eligible costs take the form of a lump sum; costs not directly related to the employment and training of the research associate are not eligible.
- Evaluation focuses on the demonstrated benefit of transnational recruitment, the excellence and impact of the innovation idea, and the coherence and effectiveness of the training and recruitment plan, which should offer equal opportunities to female and male applicants.
- Successful SMEs are expected to advertise their vacancy on the EURAXESS portal and other platforms, indicating that the grant was awarded, and to develop a work package for the recruitment campaign so related costs can be paid on completion of the work package, in line with lump-sum rules, regardless of whether recruitment succeeded.
- If projects use satellite-based earth observation, positioning, navigation or related timing data and services, Copernicus or Galileo and EGNOS must be used.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This is a company-led instrument, not a university-led one, and the honest reading is that it is not a call for Heriot-Watt or the National Robotarium to bid for directly. It is an opportunity to be captured for the portfolio of companies around the National Robotarium: the listed robotics startups and SMEs in the Industrial and Environmental Systems, Healthcare and AI and Digital and Advanced Robotics groups are exactly the innovative SMEs the action targets, and several are small enough that a funded 12-to-24-month research associate would materially change their capacity. The concrete action for the Delivery Board is to circulate the topic to the named company contacts, help each one articulate the skill shortage and the innovation idea as a vacancy notice, and position Heriot-Watt and Edinburgh as the training and development provider through European Universities alliance links — which is also a legitimate route for the universities to be involved without being the applicant.

## Points to watch

- Only individual SMEs may apply as mono-beneficiaries, so the university cannot lead. The realistic institutional role is as training provider and brokerage.
- The per-project value of 0.15 to 0.30 million euro is roughly an order of magnitude below the deck's 2-5 million euro band; the 30 million euro figure is the whole-topic pool across about 130 grants.
- Applications are 10 pages, so the effort-to-reward ratio for an SME is unusually favourable and this is a quick win to promote early.
