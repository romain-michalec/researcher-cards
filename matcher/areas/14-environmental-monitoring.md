# 14 · Environmental Monitoring & Autonomous Systems

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 14 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 14 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. Two families match — the Bridging actions climate strand and the nature-positive monitoring topics — and one of them has the earliest remaining deadline across the 16 areas.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under Climate Resilience with a 2027 window and a value of 6-12 million euro. Two horizontal families match: the Bridging actions climate strand, HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-04, and the nature-positive call HORIZON-NATURE-2027, whose monitoring topics are the closest subject match. The three topics to work from are HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-04-03, bringing innovative climate preparedness and resilience solutions closer to the market, a Coordination and Support Action; HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-04-08, sustainability and resilience of climate projections and related data records, a Coordination and Support Action; and HORIZON-NATURE-2027-02-05, cost-effective biodiversity monitoring for EU policies, a Research and Innovation Action whose scope includes automated and real-time monitoring frameworks supported by AI.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 6-12 million euro, which sits between the instruments: the climate preparedness action is 20.00 million euro for a single project; the climate projections action is 5.00 million euro for a single project; and the biodiversity monitoring topic is 16.00 million euro across three projects of 5.00 to 6.00 million euro. The biodiversity monitoring band of 5.00 to 6.00 million euro per project is the closest project-scale match.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows 2027. The climate strand of the bridging call opens on 6 October 2026 with a deadline of 16 February 2027. The biodiversity monitoring topic opens on 13 October 2026 with a deadline of 14 January 2027.

## Scope as drafted

The climate preparedness and resilience action aims to help innovative solutions, including nature-based solutions, advance in their investment journey, mobilise public authorities to stimulate demand for climate adaptation solutions, and identify structural and operational bottlenecks to deployment along with good practices and policy-relevant insight. It responds to the recognition that climate preparedness is an emerging economic opportunity, and that as impacts intensify through heatwaves, floods, droughts and supply-chain disruption, the ability to anticipate, adapt and respond becomes decisive for productivity, investment attractiveness and long-term growth. Climate resilience technologies are described as key to Europe's strategic autonomy, by embedding resilience into industrial, agricultural, maritime and forest-based, civil security, health, mobility and urban systems.

The project must provide financial support to third parties to bring at least 25 solutions closer to market, aimed at solutions already relatively close to market at TRL 7, with priority to at least one solution in each of seven areas: water, food and agriculture, transport, energy, health, civil security and critical infrastructure. Support covers business development including strategic and financial planning and regulatory guidance; innovation acceleration including final product development and capacity building; market deployment preparation including market knowledge, commercialisation support and regulatory compliance; and market-ready pilots including pilot implementation and performance testing. The consortium must pool demand and carry out buyer mapping and demand aggregation, engaging public authorities including those involved in the EU Mission on Adaptation to Climate Change, the Covenant of Mayors and the New European Bauhaus. At least 80 per cent of the requested EU contribution should go to financial support to third parties, the maximum per third party is 800,000 euro, only private for-profit entities established in a Member State or associated country are eligible as third parties, and the first cascade call should be launched in the first 12 months.

The climate projections action aims to strengthen EU climate data and infrastructure sovereignty, secure access to essential observation and modelling data for the European research community, deliver climate information relevant to European and global policy including timely contributions to IPCC assessments and UNFCCC processes, and strengthen the resilience of the Earth System Grid Federation in Europe and globally while laying the foundations for an integrated sustainable long-term operational solution. The action should track the availability of climate change projections and related data records and the evolution of global compensation efforts, gaps and risks in the context of disengagement by major partners; coordinate Europe-wide solutions to mitigate the most pressing risks in securing access to data needed for IPCC, WCRP, CMIP and CORDEX; and improve the resilience and development of the Earth System Grid Federation, through a European route preparing the ground towards a sustainable long-term solution in coordination with national efforts and in the context of ESFRI, including mobilising sustained national investment in storage, staff and core resources, and through a global coordination function sustaining access, data availability, redundancy and maintenance with a strong focus on inclusiveness, equity and the needs of the most vulnerable countries.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Climate preparedness and resilience solutions, including nature-based solutions, advance in their investment journey and get closer to market, strengthening the competitiveness of the companies and organisations developing them.
- Public authorities are mobilised to stimulate demand for climate adaptation solutions, contributing to the development and maturity of the European resilience market.
- Structural and operational bottlenecks to the deployment of climate resilience solutions are identified, along with good practices, and policy-relevant insights are derived.
- EU climate data and infrastructure sovereignty is strengthened; climate information is delivered effectively for European and global policy including IPCC and UNFCCC; and the resilience of the Earth System Grid Federation is strengthened with foundations laid for a sustainable long-term operational solution.
- For the biodiversity monitoring topic: more precise and more efficient monitoring schemes building on advanced technologies and more abundant data, and better tools for authorities implementing and evaluating EU environmental legislation.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Climate preparedness and resilience action: Coordination and Support Action, 20.00 million euro for one project; beneficiaries must provide financial support to third parties, capped at 800,000 euro per third party, with third parties restricted to private for-profit entities established in a Member State or associated country; at least 80 per cent of the EU contribution to be used for that support; first cascade call within 12 months; solutions target TRL 7.
- Climate projections action: Coordination and Support Action, 5.00 million euro for one project; eligible entities established in low- to middle-income countries automatically eligible for Horizon Europe funding may exceptionally participate as beneficiary or affiliated entity; lump-sum financing.
- Biodiversity monitoring topic: Research and Innovation Action, 16.00 million euro across three projects of 5.00 to 6.00 million euro; balanced-portfolio procedure guaranteeing the highest-ranked proposal in each of areas A, B and C; lump-sum financing; FAIR data with EOSC and eLTER considered; cooperation with the EC Knowledge Centre for Biodiversity and the Science Service for Biodiversity resourced.
- Copernicus or Galileo and EGNOS is mandatory where satellite-based earth observation, positioning, navigation or related timing data and services are used.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This area is one of the better fits across the 16 areas for autonomous environmental monitoring, because the biodiversity monitoring topic explicitly requires automated and real-time monitoring frameworks supported by AI and the climate projects topic requires satellite, ground, air and in-situ observation capability.

On the company side, Cyberhawk on aerial inspection and monitoring, Novosound on ultrasonic sensing, Frontier Robotics, HonuWorx, SeeByte and Coda Octopus on autonomous marine platforms and subsea sensing, and Flowcopter on uncrewed rotorcraft are all directly relevant. The realistic structure is a lead role on the biodiversity monitoring topic in Area C, the Water Framework Directive, where autonomous survey platforms and AI-based identification meet the topic's requirements, with a partner role on the larger climate instruments.

## Points to watch

- The two climate instruments are Coordination and Support Actions, not research actions: one is a cascading fund for businesses and one is a data-infrastructure coordination action. Neither funds autonomous systems development directly.
- The biodiversity monitoring topic is the only one of the three that funds monitoring technology development, and it requires DNA library work alongside the sensing and AI work.
- The 14 January 2027 deadline on the nature-positive call is earlier than almost everything else in this pack and leaves little consortium-building time from an autumn 2026 decision.
