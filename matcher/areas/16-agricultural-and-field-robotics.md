# 16 · Agricultural & Field Robotics — Autonomous Operations

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 16 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 16 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. There is no field robotics topic in Horizontal Activities; the nearest matches are ecological monitoring and restoration, both on the earliest remaining deadline across the 16 areas.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under Agricultural and Field Robotics with a 2027 window and a value of 5-10 million euro. There is no agricultural or field robotics topic in Horizontal Activities. Three horizontal instruments carry relevant content: HORIZON-NATURE-2027-02-05, cost-effective biodiversity monitoring for EU policies, where soil biodiversity under the Soil Monitoring Law and the EU Pollinators Monitoring Scheme require field-scale automated sensing and AI-based identification; HORIZON-NATURE-2027-02-02, living labs co-creating solutions for the restoration of ecosystems, where Area B requires biodiversity-friendly farming and forestry systems, agroecological approaches including agroforestry and grazing-based grassland, decentralised farmer- and forester-informed data systems and tools for in-situ conservation of genetic resources; and HORIZON-RAISE-2027-01-02, the food-focused automated scientific discovery topic, at 3.00 million euro for a single project, which addresses laboratory automation applied to biomass and precision fermentation, alternative proteins and food ingredients.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 5-10 million euro. The living labs topic funds at around 10.00 million euro per project across six projects in a 60.00 million euro budget, which matches the deck's upper bound; the biodiversity monitoring topic funds at 5.00 to 6.00 million euro per project across three projects in a 16.00 million euro budget; and the food-focused automated discovery topic funds 3.00 million euro for a single project, below the deck's range.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows 2027. The living labs and biodiversity monitoring topics sit in the call groups that open on 13 October 2026 with a deadline of 14 January 2027, and the food-focused automated discovery topic opens on 13 October 2026 with a deadline of 2 February 2027.

## Scope as drafted

The living labs topic, HORIZON-NATURE-2027-02-02, requires proposals to support the set-up of three new living labs with 8 to 15 experimental sites each across different ecosystems, as places for co-creating, testing and demonstrating innovative restoration solutions on three principles: co-creation with a large and balanced set of stakeholders including researchers, land and water managers such as farmers and foresters and owners, policy decision makers, economic actors, civil society, social partners, municipalities and regions, with adequate representation of primary-sector actors; activities carried out in real-life settings; and involvement of stakeholders including practitioners at all stages of development. Proposals must cover different geographical regions or climate zones and at least three different Member States or associated countries including sites in outermost regions, address the gender dimension, and establish a work plan for co-design, co-development and co-implementation. Living labs must act as real-life policy experimentation environments testing regulatory, financial and governance innovations with active involvement of competent public authorities and feedback mechanisms to inform policy-making. Proposals must assess ecosystem baselines, anticipate restoration trajectories and establish sustainable monitoring, establish harmonised Key Performance Indicators and measurement, reporting and verification protocols compatible with the Nature Restoration Regulation and the Water Resilience Strategy, identify lighthouses for training and peer learning, and define sustainability pathways, governance structures and business models beyond the project.

Proposals must address Area A, building risk-resilience with restoration, or Area B, developing effective and viable restoration practices in agriculture or forestry. Under Area A at least one living lab must address water resilience against floods and droughts through restoration of natural connectivity and water retention, and at least one must address fire risk management in agriculture and forestry with cost-benefit analysis and avoided-loss assessments. Under Area B, sites should be selected along a gradient of practice intensity, measuring restoration potential through MRV protocols; at least one living lab must develop, test and validate tools for in-situ conservation of genetic resources and sustainable use of agro-biodiversity or forest biodiversity, with a focus on decentralised, farmer- and forester-informed data systems and standard protocols for local genetic resource maintenance and exchange; and at least one must integrate a strong focus on financing restoration, including monitoring and verification methods to assess biodiversity impacts of carbon farming practices in the context of the Carbon Removal and Carbon Farming framework and their interaction with biodiversity support schemes including emerging nature credit mechanisms. Financial support to third parties may be used to facilitate involvement of small actors such as land managers, SMEs or civil society, capped at a maximum of 30 per cent of the proposed budget. Proposals must apply the multi-actor approach.

The biodiversity monitoring topic's Area B is the most directly field-robotics-relevant strand in Horizontal Activities: it requires soil biodiversity monitoring for the Soil Monitoring Law, where Member States must monitor the diversity of fungi and bacteria in soil through DNA metabarcoding and optionally archaea, protists and other soil organisms, and requires completion of DNA libraries for the main groups of fungi and bacteria with substantial advances for archaea, protists and other relevant soil organisms. Area A requires completion of DNA libraries for key pollinator groups including bees, hoverflies, butterflies and moths, and substantial advances for other pollinating insects. Both areas require scalable approaches, demonstration cases, automated and real-time monitoring frameworks supported by AI where possible, and engagement with end users to tailor approaches to their needs.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Stakeholders are empowered to co-create, deploy and replicate viable ecosystem restoration measures with wider uptake, with solutions to implementation bottlenecks and financing issues.
- Competent authorities implementing the Nature Restoration Regulation can design and reuse effective deployment frameworks informed by evidence and lessons from living labs across contexts.
- Social, economic and environmental co-benefits, risks and trade-offs of nature restoration are demonstrated and made explicit to support informed decision-making and scaling.
- For the biodiversity monitoring topic: monitoring schemes that are more precise and more efficient, and monitoring tools that are more user-friendly for authorities implementing and evaluating EU environmental legislation.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Living labs topic: Research and Innovation Action, 60.00 million euro across six projects of around 10.00 million euro; multi-actor approach mandatory; balanced-portfolio procedure guaranteeing the two highest-ranked proposals in each of areas A and B; lump-sum financing; financial support to third parties permitted up to 60,000 euro per third party and capped at 30 per cent of the proposed budget; networking obligation with the coordination action under HORIZON-NATURE-2027-02-03.
- Biodiversity monitoring topic: Research and Innovation Action, 16.00 million euro across three projects of 5.00 to 6.00 million euro; balanced-portfolio procedure for areas A, B and C; lump-sum financing; FAIR data with EOSC and eLTER considered.
- Food-focused automated discovery: Research and Innovation Action, 3.00 million euro for one project, prioritising software with complementary equipment permitted, with international collaboration encouraged; Chinese-entity participation barred as a RAISE topic.
- Multi-actor projects must summarise new knowledge in practice abstracts in the common EIP-AGRI format for Horizon and upload them to the EIP-AGRI project database, and are strongly recommended to involve interactive innovation groups such as EIP-AGRI operational groups funded under the Common Agricultural Policy.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

Field and agricultural robotics is one of the clearest applied-robotics opportunities across the 16 areas, because the biodiversity monitoring and living lab topics both need field-deployable sensing and data systems and the multi-actor requirement means the university is expected to be one partner among farmers, foresters, land managers and authorities rather than the sole driver. Heriot-Watt's contribution would come from its autonomy and perception groups, with complementary strengths at the University of Edinburgh.

The industrial partners from the National Robotarium list are thinner here than for the marine area: Frontier Robotics and Flowcopter offer field-capable platforms, Novosound offers sensing, and Cyberhawk offers aerial survey; the agricultural and environmental monitoring partners will most likely have to come from outside the current list, alongside agricultural and forestry stakeholders recruited for the multi-actor requirement. Realistically the National Robotarium's play is a platform and autonomy work package inside a living lab consortium, or the AI and sensing strand of a monitoring bid in Area B on soils — where soil sampling, in-situ sensing and automated identification of soil organisms are the deliverable.

## Points to watch

- No horizontal topic matches agricultural and field robotics as a subject. The area's nearest matches are ecological monitoring and restoration, so the proposal must be framed around environmental outcomes.
- The multi-actor approach is mandatory on the living labs topic and imposes a consortium shape that a technology-led bid can find constraining; the practice-abstract and EIP-AGRI dissemination requirements are additional administrative load.
- The 14 January 2027 deadline applies to both the living labs and monitoring topics, which is the earliest remaining deadline across the 16 areas and leaves very little consortium-building time.
- Financial support to third parties is capped as a share of budget on the living labs topic, which needs to be built into the budget model from the start.
