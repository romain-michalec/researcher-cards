# 8 · Robotics Research Platforms

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 8 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 8 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. The research infrastructure cluster inside Bridging actions is the honest match, and the six target areas are fixed in advance.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under Research Infrastructure with a 2027 window and a value of 10-30 million euro. Horizontal Activities does contain a substantive research infrastructure cluster inside the Bridging actions call, and three topics in it are directly relevant: HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-06, access to research infrastructure services to address industry needs in priority areas; HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-07, Europe's world-class research and technology infrastructures as strategic assets for talent to Choose Europe; and HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-04, the long-term resilience and European sovereignty of critical research data and knowledge repositories and scientific services for the life sciences.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 10-30 million euro. The work programme numbers are: HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-06, 15.00 million euro total with around 5.00 million euro per project and three projects; HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-07, 7.50 million euro total with around 1.50 million euro per project and five projects; HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-04, 8.00 million euro for one project. The whole of the HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03 call is 30.50 million euro, which is the call total rather than a project value. The deck's upper bound therefore corresponds to the whole call.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows 2027. The HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03 call opens on 9 March 2027 with a deadline of 15 June 2027. The six target areas in the access topic are fixed in advance, which means the funding is real but the subject must be one of those areas.

## Scope as drafted

The access topic, HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-06, provides trans-national access, on-site or remote, and virtual access to integrated and customised research infrastructure services for industry-driven research, offered by complementary state-of-the-art research infrastructures, in six target areas: Area 1 Biomedical technologies; Area 2 Clean energy and clean mobility solutions; Area 3 Advanced materials for circular economy and bioeconomy; Area 4 Accelerators and superconducting magnets; Area 5 Microelectronics; Area 6 Advanced sensing technologies for ocean observation. Proposals must address one area and identify it clearly. Access should target users from industry, notably SMEs, startups and scaleups, or from academia when collaborating with industry. Access includes ad hoc user training and scientific and technical support, and appropriate support to technical and scientific staff who facilitate access by non-expert industry users should be considered.

The primary goal is access provision to existing services, which should be reflected in activities and allocated resources; development and customisation of services or access modalities will also be supported provided the resulting services are opened and offered under the action and their long-term sustainability is ensured. Proposals should pay attention to shortening the time to access for industry users, address data management and related ethics in line with FAIR principles, and connect digital services to the European Open Science Cloud where relevant. Proposals should include at least one ESFRI Landmark or European Research Infrastructure Consortium as beneficiary.

The talent topic, HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-07, is a Coordination and Support Action at 7.50 million euro across five projects of around 1.50 million euro each. It aims to leverage Europe's research and technology infrastructures to attract global scientific talent and strengthen Europe as a partner of choice for international scientific cooperation. Projects should partner with non-associated third-country scientific institutions including universities to align research methodologies, testing protocols and data sharing; promote uptake in other world regions of practices developed by pan-European infrastructures to ensure comparability of results; and support the design of distributed research ecosystems including data and cloud platforms. Joint workshops, training programmes, summer schools and scientific visits are expected, targeted at students, early-career researchers and scientific staff, with gender-balanced participation. Proposals are expected to address one of nine listed areas, which include a cross-cutting area and an other area, and consortia must include at least one ESFRI Landmark or ERIC as beneficiary.

The life-science data topic, HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-03-04, is a Research and Innovation Action of 8.00 million euro for one project. It aims to increase the resilience and sovereignty of critical European research data and knowledge repositories and scientific services used in the life sciences, particularly biomedical and biomolecular disciplines; to further integrate trusted FAIR data and research infrastructures into the European Open Science Cloud Federation; and to increase European leadership in international collaboration frameworks supporting data infrastructures. Proposals should reinforce integration into the EOSC Federation with fully operational adoption of federating capabilities and concrete use cases demonstrating economies of scale across EOSC Nodes; develop and deploy solutions and collaborations that let the broader community sustain and enrich repositories, strengthening technical reliability and reducing service interruption risk; develop and demonstrate business and financing models that minimise the risk of service downgrades; support participation in European or global collaborative initiatives such as the Global Biodata Coalition; and support training and upskilling for technical and scientific staff to avoid loss of critical expertise.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Research infrastructures improve their attractiveness for industry and can sustainably extend their user base, with future continuity with technology infrastructures facilitated.
- Researchers and innovators get more efficient access to the best research infrastructures irrespective of location, through a single-entry-point access portal, integrated or interoperable catalogues of services and converging access conditions.
- Breakthrough research is enabled by advanced infrastructure services made available to a wider user community in the targeted areas.
- A new generation of researchers and innovators is trained to exploit the essential tools for industry-driven research, with attention to early-career researchers and those from widening countries.
- Increased resilience and sovereignty of critical European research data and knowledge repositories and scientific services, with further integration of FAIR data into the EOSC Federation and increased European leadership internationally.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Access topic: Research and Innovation Action, 15.00 million euro across three projects of around 5.00 million euro, page limit 100 pages, access provision activities mandatory, at least one ESFRI Landmark or ERIC expected as beneficiary, Joint Research Centre may join with zero funding, award criteria extended for access quality and integration, additional royalty-free access rights obligations, unit-cost financing available for trans-national and virtual access.
- Talent topic: Coordination and Support Action, 7.50 million euro across five projects of around 1.50 million euro; eligible non-associated third-country entities may exceptionally participate; at least one ESFRI Landmark or ERIC required.
- Life-science data topic: Research and Innovation Action, 8.00 million euro for one project; subject to restrictions for the protection of European communication networks; award thresholds raised to 4/4/4 with a cumulative 12; lump-sum financing.
- All three topics require the use of Copernicus or Galileo and EGNOS if satellite-based earth observation, positioning, navigation or related timing data and services are used.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This area is where the National Robotarium's own research platform story should be told, and the honest position is that the horizontal instruments fund access to and integration of existing infrastructures rather than the construction of new ones. The realistic play is Area 6, advanced sensing technologies for ocean observation, which is a direct fit with the autonomous systems and marine robotics community at Heriot-Watt and the University of Edinburgh, and with the Ocean Eye and Global Ocean Observing System content in the nature-positive call. Area 2, clean energy and clean mobility, and Area 3, advanced materials for circular economy and bioeconomy, are the other plausible framings.

For a National Robotarium platform to be funded as an access provider, it would need to be presented as a European facility serving industry users, with a robust access scheme, trained support staff, quality assurance and long-term sustainability, and with an ESFRI Landmark or ERIC in the consortium. The talent topic is the cheaper route to visibility and is a good fit for the applied robotics skills agenda.

## Points to watch

- The access topic's six areas are fixed. A robotics platform bid must sit inside one of them; there is no general robotics infrastructure area.
- An ESFRI Landmark or ERIC must be a beneficiary in the talent topic and should be one in the access topic, so leading alone is not realistic.
- Access provision is the deliverable, not facility construction — the value band in the deck will not be met by these instruments.
