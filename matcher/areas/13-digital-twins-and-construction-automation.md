# 13 · Digital Twins & Construction Automation

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 13 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 13 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. The same Clean Industrial Deal families apply as for area 12, and the 2026 vintage has already closed.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under the Clean Industrial Deal with a window from autumn 2026 and a value of 6-15 million euro. The same two families apply as for area 12: the Clean Industrial Deal decarbonisation topics and the Clean Technologies for Climate Action topics. Within those, the content that supports a digital twin and construction automation framing is the circularity and resource efficiency area and the integrated net-zero emissions energy systems area, where proposals may address step-change in network and infrastructure deployment and the application of advanced materials development, connected process engineering and scale-up.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 6-15 million euro. The underlying topics fund at 15.00 to 25.00 million euro per project, so the deck's upper bound only reaches the smallest expected contribution. The 2026 and 2027 clean technology topics carry 150.00 million euro and 140.00 million euro respectively at 15.00 to 25.00 million euro per project across eight projects each, and the energy-intensive industry topics 125.00 million euro each at the same per-project range.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows autumn 2026 onwards. The 2026 call opened on 18 December 2025 with a deadline of 15 September 2026 and has closed; the 2027 call opens on 12 January 2027 with a deadline of 15 September 2027.

## Scope as drafted

The clean technology topic, HORIZON-CID-2027-01-02, asks proposals to strengthen the competitiveness, sustainability and resilience of an innovative clean tech solution by demonstrating the capability to significantly increase its circular material use rate against a sound and realistic baseline; to reduce the levelised cost of energy delivered to end users, including where relevant production, distribution and storage costs, against a realistic baseline and across different geographic scenarios; and to contribute to Europe's industrial leadership and competitiveness in line with the Net-Zero Industry Act objectives for EU manufacturing capacity of net-zero technologies. It also asks proposals to bring the solution to full technological maturity and close to market readiness to accelerate deployment or integration in key industrial sectors in Europe such as manufacturing, energy and transport.

The three clean tech areas in scope are integrated net-zero emissions energy systems, including energy grids, networks and systems; enhanced zero-emission power technologies, including renewable electricity, heat and energy technologies; and storage technologies, renewable fuels and carbon capture and utilisation, including batteries and other energy storage, renewable hydrogen, advanced biofuels and synthetic renewable fuels. Proposals must explicitly select one main area but may combine them, and applicants are free to decide which value chain to strengthen. Proposals may also address step-change in network and infrastructure deployment to facilitate scale-up across trans-European energy and transport networks — electricity, heat, gas, hydrogen, carbon dioxide, batteries and refuelling networks — and may address advanced innovative materials development, connected process engineering and scale-up, resource efficiency, circularity and recycling. Attention should be paid to avoiding technologies that may have a negative impact on biodiversity and ecosystems.

Proposals must ensure the development of innovative technological solutions along a specific clean tech value chain, involving an adequate combination of clean tech suppliers, energy users in manufacturing, energy and transport, and other relevant stakeholders, to support a sound business case and take a gender-sensitive and intersectional approach. They must demonstrate adequate integration of relevant technological solutions and a clear, quantifiable impact on competitiveness and greenhouse gas reduction, and show industrial leadership in deployment after the project, with an industry-driven consortium of a manageable number of participants, indicatively not more than ten, with the size justified against the extent of the value chain covered. They should seek coherence with the work of the Strategic Energy Technology Plan and relevant industrial initiatives. The business plan and market-readiness strategy requirements and the go/no-go moment are as set out for area 12.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Strengthened competitiveness, sustainability and resilience of an innovative clean tech solution, demonstrated through an increased circular material use rate, reduced levelised cost of energy delivered to end users, and contribution to Europe's industrial leadership in line with the Net-Zero Industry Act.
- The solution brought to full technological maturity and close to market readiness, accelerating market deployment or integration in key industrial sectors in Europe.
- For the call as a whole: accelerated roll-out of decarbonised and clean tech solutions, improved European competitiveness, resilience and strategic autonomy, mobilised public and private investment, and a reduced energy price gap.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Innovation Action; TRL 6 at start rising to TRL 8 by the end for the clean technology topic, and TRL 7 to 8 for the energy-intensive industry topic.
- Page limit exceptionally extended to a maximum of 60 pages in part B.
- Award procedure gives at least one grant to the highest-ranked application in each of the three areas, provided thresholds are met.
- 2027 vintage: entities controlled by China or by a Chinese legal entity are not eligible to participate; the granting authority may object to transfer of ownership or exclusive licensing for up to four years after the action ends.
- Consortium size indicatively not more than ten participants; SME participation encouraged.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

A digital twins and construction automation bid needs a genuine built-environment and energy-systems partner, and this is where Heriot-Watt's breadth beyond robotics is an advantage. The University of Edinburgh adds a wider group on digital twins and data-driven systems. On the company side, Utopi on building monitoring and Singul8 on automation are the nearest fits from the National Robotarium list, with Cyberhawk and Novosound relevant for asset inspection and sensing.

The honest assessment is that the topic is a clean technology and industrial decarbonisation topic, and a construction-automation bid will only succeed if it is anchored on a demonstrator that decarbonises a real industrial or infrastructure system with a quantified case. The go/no-go gate and the permitted-demonstrator requirement mean the industrial lead has to be secured before submission.

## Points to watch

- The deck's 6-15 million euro band sits below the 15-25 million euro per-project range of the underlying topics; the deck's value is understated rather than overstated, but the bid cost is correspondingly higher.
- The 2026 vintage has closed. Only the 2027 call, opening 12 January 2027, remains.
- There is no dedicated digital twin or construction topic; the framing has to be justified inside clean technology area 1 or 3.
- The Net-Zero Industry Act manufacturing-capacity conditions, including sourcing expectations for components where EU supply is below 50 per cent, add a condition that partner screening must pick up.
