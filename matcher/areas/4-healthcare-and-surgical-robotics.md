# 4 · Healthcare & Surgical Robotics

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 4 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 4 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. There is no matching topic in Horizontal Activities — this area belongs to the mainstream Cluster 1 health work programme, and the nearest horizontal instruments are set out below.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under Cluster 1 Health, which sits in the mainstream Cluster work programme rather than in Horizontal Activities: the Horizontal Activities part contains no Cluster 1 topic. The nearest horizontal instruments that carry surgical and clinical robotics content are the AI in Science Automated Scientific Discovery topic, whose stated impact areas include drug discovery, and the One Health biodiversity and health interface topic, HORIZON-NATURE-2027-03-01. What follows sets out the wider health-relevant conditions that apply across Horizontal Activities, and the specific conditions of the health-adjacent horizontal topic, so the area can be assessed honestly rather than presented with detail the source does not contain.

## Scope as drafted

Within Horizontal Activities, the health-relevant scope is concentrated in the One Health topic. It asks for interdisciplinary, data-driven research at the biodiversity and health interface, covering three groups of work. The first is thematic use cases: a minimum of six, which together must address the most significant direct drivers of biodiversity loss — land and sea use change, direct exploitation, climate change, pollution of air, water and soil, and invasive alien and proliferating species — and their impacts on health, with outcomes including but not limited to human health, infectious diseases and non-communicable diseases including mental health. Use cases involving infectious disease must consider the wildlife and domesticated-animal interface, including multi-host pathogens and spillover. Together the use cases must cover at least three different ecosystem types across terrestrial, freshwater and marine realms and different biogeographical regions.

The second group is data sharing and interoperability: selecting and mobilising existing datasets across domains, identifying critical data gaps including in view of European data sovereignty, promoting inter- and transdisciplinary research between life sciences and environmental domains through the European Open Science Cloud Federation and other common European data spaces, developing and validating analytical and data source integration tools including through AI models, building harmonised ontologies and reporting standards, and providing data and software governance recommendations that embed FAIR and, where relevant, CARE principles.

The third group is a proof of concept for a holistic One Health approach, built on the use cases, demonstrating how holistic approaches such as One Health or Planetary Health can enhance resilience to biodiversity loss, ecosystem degradation, disease and pests, pollution and climate change, and producing evidence-based actionable outputs such as recommendations, guidelines, interoperable indicators and decision-support tools.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Public authorities, researchers and policymakers have robust knowledge foundations and analytical frameworks that enable inter- and transdisciplinary biodiversity-related research, address interdependencies and strengthen operationalisation of One Health and Planetary Health.
- Researchers, public authorities and global organisations have access to FAIR data supported by enhanced digital infrastructures, building on improved identification, mobilisation and interoperability of data across ecosystem and wildlife health, the main drivers of biodiversity loss and their impacts on human and animal health.
- A wide set of stakeholders can assess the health benefits and risk prevention associated with maintaining, improving and restoring ecosystem health, and are supported in applying evidence-based recommendations, tools and solutions.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Research and Innovation Action; 20.00 million euro for a single project; application page limit 70 pages.
- Consortium composition is unusually prescriptive: at least seven independent legal entities, at least one established in a Member State and at least six others each in different Member States or associated countries.
- Award criteria thresholds are raised to 4 for Excellence, 4 for Impact and 4 for Implementation, with a cumulative threshold of 12.
- The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control, the European Food Safety Authority, the European Environment Agency or another relevant decentralised EU agency responsible for human, animal and environmental risk assessment may participate as consortium members without funding, and the Joint Research Centre may participate with zero funding or as an associated partner. Neither participates in proposal preparation.
- Proposals using satellite-based earth observation, positioning, navigation or related timing data must use Copernicus or Galileo and EGNOS, though other data and services may be used additionally.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

The medical robotics capability that the deck implies for this area sits with Heriot-Watt's medical and assistive robotics community, with complementary strength at the University of Edinburgh. The listed healthcare-facing companies, BIOLIBERTY (Rowan Armstrong), Neuranics, Solasta Bio and PneumoWave, are the natural industrial partners.

Given that the horizontal part does not carry a surgical robotics topic, the practical route for this area is either the Cluster 1 work programme directly, or a surgical and clinical robotics framing inside the AI in Science automated discovery topic, where closed-loop experimentation and robotic laboratory or clinical systems are explicitly in scope, or a health-data framing inside the One Health topic. The Delivery Board should record that this area is currently without a matched horizontal call.

## Points to watch

- No horizontal call topic matches this area. The eight to twenty million euro value band is not evidenced in the source document.
- If the One Health topic is used as the vehicle, the proposal must be genuinely biodiversity-anchored with a mandatory link to human health; a pure surgical robotics bid will not fit.
- Consortium size rules in the One Health topic are more demanding than in most other Horizontal Activities topics.
