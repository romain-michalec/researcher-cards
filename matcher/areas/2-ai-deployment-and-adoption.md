# 2 · AI Deployment & Adoption

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 2 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 2 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. The call family named in the deck does not contain a topic of this description, so the mapping has been corrected here against the Apply AI topics.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this area under call family HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01. The Bridging actions call HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01 does exist, but none of its topics is an AI deployment and adoption topic as such. Its four topics are HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-09 (EU AI Media Platform), HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-10 (Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities), HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-12 (large-scale piloting of next-generation AI-driven digital platforms for the energy system) and HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-13 (EU Frontier AI Initiative: Advanced Scaling Frameworks for High-Performance AI Models). The horizontal instrument that actually matches an AI deployment and adoption ambition is the group of four Apply AI topics just listed, in the sense that they exist to move AI from research into real operations across sectors.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 6-12 million euro. The Bridging actions call HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01 has an overall indicative budget of 55.00 million euro, spread across four topics each worth 10.00 or 15.00 million euro with a single project expected per topic. Whole-call value therefore exceeds the deck's range, but the largest individual grant is 15.00 million euro and all four topics are single-project competitions.

Two indicative values that matter most for partner decisions: HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-10 (Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities), 15.00 million euro for one Innovation Action; HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-09 (EU AI Media Platform), 10.00 million euro for one Innovation Action. Both are expected to fund a single project.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows Q4 2026 to Q3 2027. The Work Programme gives the call an opening on 6 October 2026 and a deadline of 18 March 2027.

## Scope as drafted

The Bridging actions call as a whole exists to ease the transition from Horizon Europe 2021-2027 to the successor framework programme and to the European Competitiveness Fund, around a concept the Work Programme calls the innovation to investment journey — a seamless process from research to market deployment, responding to the diagnosis that Europe's weakness lies at commercialisation rather than at research. The call deliberately covers three main areas: knowledge valorisation and data access; research infrastructures and data access; and artificial intelligence.

The AI strand is where the deployment and adoption logic sits. The Work Programme describes these Apply AI actions as an innovative approach to structurally support cross-domain priorities enabling AI-based innovation in the key sectors identified in the Apply AI Strategy, addressing immediate sectoral needs while contributing to the policy priorities of the next framework programme. The topics target urgent actions to address market failures and reduce strategic dependencies, on the argument that many high-potential AI solutions fail to reach the market or scale because of persistent sector-specific barriers. The actions focus on identifying and addressing those systemic obstacles, including through piloting dissemination mechanisms such as platforms or marketplaces for AI solutions and testing AI technologies, including in real-world conditions, to generate the insight needed to overcome barriers to scale.

Of the four topics, the two with the clearest deployment character are set out below.

## Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities

The topic is an Innovation Action worth 15.00 million euro for one project, expected to achieve TRL 8 by the end. It asks for development and demonstration of AI-enabled electric autonomous mobility services at level 4 in urban traffic, improving safety, sustainability, data sharing and commercial scalability across EU cities, through a large-scale demonstrator integrating five to seven coordinated city pilots in five or more Member States or associated countries, including at least one cross-border pilot between adjacent EU cities.

Cities are expected to partner with European autonomous mobility service providers and operators, vehicle manufacturers, suppliers and technology providers, and research and innovation entities, to deploy AI-enabled electric autonomous vehicles at SAE level 4 for operations such as autonomous shuttle services, ride hailing, car sharing and autonomous point-to-point navigation. Proposals should build on ongoing European, national or regional actions and seek complementary public funding and industrial investment for actual deployment at scale. The pilots should include at least one cross-border pilot to demonstrate interoperability across different national regulations and physical and digital infrastructures; mandatory sharing of data, AI models and experience across all pilots through a European neutral, secure and trusted data handling entity or similar; and attention to scalability across varied urban morphologies and regulatory contexts, with cybersecurity and interoperability requirements for data and model sharing. Proposers are expected to comply with EU AI, data and cybersecurity policy objectives including for connected and automated vehicles, to interact closely with the European Connected and Autonomous Vehicle Alliance and its working group on autonomous driving deployment, and to apply the European Common Evaluation Methodology for CCAM. Cities are encouraged to come from the Call for Expression of Interest launched in summer 2026 under the Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities initiative.

## EU Frontier AI Initiative: Advanced Scaling Frameworks

This is a Research and Innovation Action worth 15.00 million euro for one project, expected to reach TRL 4-5. It aims to advance the scientific and technical foundations for scaling AI models, enhancing training and inference performance and giving European AI laboratories the methodologies needed to compete at the global frontier. Outcomes sought include robust scaling laws and predictive frameworks that allow reliable estimation of model performance before large-scale compute investment; improved competitiveness of European advanced AI models through optimised dataset mixtures, refined architectures and better hyperparameter configurations; and optimised development of frontier models through automated and scientifically grounded scaling methodologies.

Research areas include automated scaling-law derivation that predicts performance across modalities and tasks beyond simple power laws; exploratory architecture search for methods that maintain performance while scaling training and inference, including sparsity, memory efficiency and long-context handling; dataset mixture optimisation covering systematic selection, weighting and sequencing of data plus automated data quality assessment; and advanced hyperparameter tuning and stability for stable training at large scale. Frameworks must be compatible with diverse hardware architectures to avoid optimisation for specific proprietary hardware. The selected project is expected to support advanced AI laboratories and high-performance computing centres already developing frontier AI, to describe how the methodologies benefit models adapted to strategic sectors such as public administration, manufacturing, cultural and creative industries or physical AI, to share communicable results through the AI-on-demand platform, to link to the resources offered by AI Factories and Data Labs, and to allocate tasks for cohesion with the European Partnership on AI, data and robotics and with the GenAI4EU central Hub. It complements HORIZON-CL4-2027-04-DIGITAL-EMERGING-11.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

For the autonomous mobility topic specifically: delivery on the Apply AI Strategy flagship action Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities by bridging the gap between high technical maturity and sustainable commercial deployment in EU cities; strengthened competitiveness of European mobility service providers, OEMs and technology developers through real-world deployment opportunities; safer, more sustainable and accessible urban mobility through electric autonomous vehicles; demonstration of scalable autonomous mobility services in real traffic; improved access to and secure sharing of data, AI models and tools; demonstration of scalable supporting infrastructure; and action on the regulatory, operational and governance barriers to scaling, including procurement frameworks and capacity building within cities.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Legal entities established in China are not eligible to participate in both the Research and Innovation Actions and the Innovation Actions falling under the Bridging actions call.
- Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities: Innovation Action; TRL 8 by the end; one project expected; five to seven city pilots across at least five Member States or associated countries with at least one cross-border pilot; application of the EU Common Evaluation Methodology for CCAM.
- EU Frontier AI Initiative: Research and Innovation Action; TRL 4-5 by the end; one project expected; methods must be hardware-agnostic.
- EU AI Media Platform: Innovation Action; TRL 6 at start rising to TRL 8; 10.00 million euro for one project; subject to restrictions for the protection of European communication networks.
- Energy system digital platforms: Innovation Action; TRL 7-8 by the end; 15.00 million euro for one project; approximately 36 months; operational pilots in at least seven Member States (the Work Programme also says at least six in the next paragraph) with at least six energy actors, at least eight digital technology providers, at least four mobility actors and at least twenty energy communities.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

Autonomous Drive Ambition Cities is the closer fit of the two for an autonomous-systems group, and the harder one to lead: it is a single large demonstrator that will be assembled around city authorities and vehicle OEMs. Heriot-Watt's realistic play is as an autonomy, perception, validation and safety partner, and the University of Edinburgh brings complementary strength through Edinburgh's connected and autonomous vehicle research. The National Robotarium list offers several natural consortium members — Flowcopter (Peter McCurry) and Frontier Robotics (David Wavell) on autonomous platforms, SeeByte and Coda Octopus on marine autonomy and sensing, and RoboK on perception. The commercialisation and end-user requirements in the topic text also make the listed Scottish SMEs attractive as consortium members rather than as suppliers.

The Frontier AI scaling topic is a methodology topic and will attract labs with frontier-model credentials; the realistic Heriot-Watt contribution is in evaluation, verification and trustworthy AI rather than in scaling law derivation.

## Points to watch

- The deck's call-family label for this area does not correspond to any AI deployment and adoption topic. The call should be re-mapped to the Apply AI topics within Bridging actions before any bid decision is taken.
- Three of the four Apply AI topics are single-project competitions. Being on the winning consortium is the only outcome short of not bidding at all, so early engagement with the lead proposers matters more than usual.
- Expect an expected contribution of around 15 million euro per topic against the deck's 6-12 million euro band.
