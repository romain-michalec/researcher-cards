# 15 · Offshore & Marine — Inspection, Maintenance & Repair

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 15 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 15 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. There is no offshore inspection topic in Horizontal Activities, but the adjacent ocean observing topic is the largest single instrument in Horizontal Activities.

## Work Programme call and topic

The deck places this under Offshore and Marine Robotics with immediate engagement and submissions likely in 2027, at a value of 8-20 million euro. There is no offshore inspection, maintenance and repair topic in Horizontal Activities. The horizontal topics that carry the nearest subject matter and scale are HORIZON-NATURE-2027-04-01, the Global Ocean Observing System, an Innovation Action of 50.00 million euro for a single project, and the research infrastructure access topic's Area 6, advanced sensing technologies for ocean observation. The general inspection and autonomy work would sit in the Cluster 5 transport and mobility work programme that the bridging topics reference, and in the Cluster 4 digital work programme.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 8-20 million euro. The ocean observing topic is 50.00 million euro for one project, well above the deck's range. The access topic offers around 5.00 million euro per project across three projects in a 15.00 million euro budget, below the deck's range. No horizontal instrument funds offshore inspection, maintenance and repair at the deck's stated value.

## Scope as drafted

The ocean observing topic is the substantial marine instrument in Horizontal Activities and its content is observing and data rather than inspection, maintenance and repair. It requires proposals to reinforce and expand GOOS governance frameworks and networks operating under the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission, focused primarily on areas beyond national jurisdiction, helping address structural gaps at global scale such as the polar regions, the deep sea and other under-observed regions of relevance to Europe, and ensuring the current system can support key services such as Copernicus Services through interoperable and timely data delivery. It requires global strategic coordination led by GOOS and strengthened integration of European biodiversity and ecosystem observing efforts into the global landscape. It requires observing system experiments, observing system simulation experiments or network optimisation to design the most reliable, sustained and cost-effective observing system across coverage, resolution, accuracy and data redundancy, strengthening the link between in-situ and remote sensing programmes and optimising data assimilation and modelling, leading to the design of a minimum viable observing system fit for climate and biodiversity predictions and assessments. It requires identification of critical technology needs including AI and co-creation with industry of a multiplatform technology roadmap including environmental, biogeochemical and biological sensors, demonstration of high-TRL sensors measuring Essential Ocean Variables with enhanced data integration and interoperability, and skills development, training and knowledge exchange across regions and communities. It also requires support to the coordination efforts of GOOS and its components, support to EU contributions to the G7 Future of the Seas and Oceans Initiative, and coherence with IOC-IODE and the Essential Biodiversity Variable framework developed under GEO BON.

The proposed actions should benefit the global ocean observing system first by maintaining full capability in the short term, then by a clear operational step-change including validation, integration and sustained deployment of fit-for-purpose observing technologies, platforms, services and data delivery workflows across the GOOS value chain, while working towards long-term sustainability and structural solutions through strengthened cooperation and funding with Member States, associated countries, international partners and global governance frameworks. Proposals should prioritise solutions that reduce critical dependencies on third countries while ensuring performance, interoperability and long-term sustainability.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Scientists, public authorities and businesses benefit from strengthened and resilient global ocean observing capacities coordinated under GOOS, particularly for critical observations for marine biodiversity and ecosystem knowledge.
- Public authorities prioritise investment in infrastructure and capacity building to advance ocean research, protect and restore ocean health and biodiversity, and provide critical services through a global observing system that is fit for purpose, cost efficient and designed across all its components.
- Increased EU leadership and strategic autonomy in global ocean observation capabilities supporting EU climate and biodiversity goals, strengthening the EU's contribution to and influence within GOOS and reducing the risk of critical dependencies, notably for key services such as Copernicus Services.
- Enhanced collaboration between observation communities, marine research infrastructures, European SMEs and technology providers, maritime industries and service operators, accelerating validation, procurement readiness, uptake and large-scale deployment of innovative ocean observing technologies and approaches, in particular for biodiversity observations, while reinforcing the long-term sustainability of the system.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Innovation Action, 50.00 million euro for a single project, page limit 100 pages.
- All international organisations are exceptionally eligible for funding.
- Entities established in an eligible country but directly or indirectly controlled by China or by a Chinese legal entity are not eligible to participate.
- If projects collect in-situ data and marine observation, beneficiaries must make them openly available through the European Marine Observation and Data Network on FAIR principles. This is a hard data-sharing condition and a significant asset for partners with survey data.
- The evaluation committee will be composed partly of representatives of EU institutions.
- Purchases of equipment, infrastructure or other assets specifically for the action, or developed as part of the action tasks, may be declared as full capitalised costs. This is an unusual and favourable provision for a consortium that needs to build or instrument platforms.
- The topic forms part of a coordination initiative between the European Space Agency and the Commission on Earth System Science under the Ocean Flagship, and successful consortia will be asked to coordinate with projects selected under the European Space Agency's FutureEO programme.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

This is the natural territory for the Ocean Systems Laboratory and the marine autonomy group, and the National Robotarium's own positioning in offshore and marine robotics is the strongest single asset for this area. On the industrial side, the National Robotarium list has an unusually deep marine cluster for a single region: SeeByte and Coda Octopus on autonomous underwater systems and sonar, HonuWorx on uncrewed and resident subsea systems, Frontier Robotics on subsea robotics, Flowcopter on heavy-lift uncrewed rotorcraft, and Zelim on offshore safety and recovery. Those six companies between them cover the inspection, intervention, sensing and platform layers that an offshore autonomy consortium needs.

The strategic point for the Delivery Board is that the ocean observing topic funds the observing system and its data rather than inspection and repair, but it funds it at a scale that makes platform development, sensor demonstration and sustained operations all declarable. It is also a single very large project, so the decision is between bidding as a partner with a defined observation-technology work package and building a wider consortium around a lead. The data-sharing obligation through EMODnet should be priced into the bid, because it affects what can be commercially reserved.

## Points to watch

- No horizontal topic matches offshore inspection, maintenance and repair as drafted. The 8-20 million euro band is not evidenced in the source, though the adjacent ocean observing topic is over twice its upper bound.
- The ocean observing topic is a single-project competition of 50 million euro; consortium politics and institutional representation on the evaluation committee will matter.
- Mandatory open data release through EMODnet is a substantial condition for any partner whose business depends on survey data.
- The topic expressly prioritises solutions that reduce dependencies on third countries, which will shape which international partners can be included.
