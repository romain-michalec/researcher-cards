# 3 · Valorisation of Horizon Results

National Robotarium · Delivery Board · Horizon Europe 2026–2027

Area 3 of 16 in the opportunity pipeline · Detail drawn from the draft Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, part 14 Horizontal Activities

This document covers area 3 of the National Robotarium's 16 Horizon Europe 2026-2027 opportunity areas. This area maps precisely onto a funded topic, and the money is largely cascaded to third parties rather than retained by the consortium.

## Work Programme call and topic

Call: Bridging actions 2027 (HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-02). Topic: HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-02-02, Valorising Horizon project results via innovation ecosystems, a Research and Innovation Action.

## Indicative value

The deck values this area at 4-8 million euro. The topic itself has a total indicative budget of 20.00 million euro with an expected EU contribution of between 2.50 and 4.00 million euro per project and five projects expected. The deck's lower bound of 4 million euro matches the ceiling of the per-project band, and its upper bound of 8 million euro is above any single project but well below the 20.00 million euro topic budget. The parent call also contains HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-02-05 (Advancing promising results from Pillar II collaborative research through the innovation journey), 32.50 million euro for up to thirteen projects of not more than 2.50 million euro each, for a call total of 52.50 million euro.

## Activity window and actual dates

The deck shows Q4 2026 to Q3 2027. The call opens on 20 October 2026 with a deadline of 28 January 2027.

## Scope as drafted

The topic addresses a structural gap between research outcomes and market- or business-oriented support. Many Horizon projects end at experimental proof of concept and laboratory validation, while most acceleration, investment or pre-commercial procurement mechanisms engage only at a later stage such as validation in relevant environments, prototyping or demonstration. As a result, the elements that de-risk innovation — identifying concrete use cases, validating results in real-world environments, defining customer segments, developing viable business models or intellectual property strategies — are often underdeveloped when funding ends. That disconnect disrupts the pipeline and limits the capacity of promising innovations to progress towards follow-on financing and deployment.

Projects are expected to cover six activity groups. First, scout and map promising innovations, startups, SMEs and exploitable results emerging from Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe, whether recently concluded or ongoing, and integrate them into the innovation pipeline with particular attention to Pillar II projects and outputs with credible innovation potential. Second, launch competitive calls targeting single Horizon projects or owners of Horizon results within their thematic area, for validation and valorisation of results, with economic support used solely for prototyping, testing, demonstrating, piloting, product validation and market replication of exploitable results in near-operational or operational environments. Third, provide guidance and facilitate access for selected candidates to real-life validation sites, including demonstration sites, living labs, testing facilities and industrial partners. Fourth, provide expert mentorship and continuous tailored support through valorisation and validation, focused on value propositions, business model development, customer or user validation, preliminary market consultations, IP strategies and exploitation strategies, preparing participants to engage with accelerators and investment. Fifth, deliver targeted training on scalability covering market and investment readiness, pre-commercial procurement pathways and deployment instruments, plus support for building entrepreneurial teams around promising results. Sixth, facilitate access to and navigation of follow-on EU funding instruments including the European Innovation Council, the European Innovation Ecosystem, Regional Innovation Valleys, European Startup and Scaleup Hubs, successors to programmes such as InvestEU, and national or regional innovation funds.

Proposals must address one of five thematic areas and say which: A) Agriculture and Food systems, B) Clean transition: energy and transport, C) Health, D) Bioeconomy, and E) Raw materials and advanced materials. Consortia made up of knowledge intermediaries — technology transfer offices, EU-supported innovation ecosystems, innovation platforms, incubators, clusters and entrepreneurship support initiatives — are described as best placed to link Horizon project consortia with accelerators, investors, municipalities, regions, clusters, procurers, living labs and mission-oriented innovation platforms.

Proposals should provide financial support to third parties, and because the primary purpose is de-risking through validation, a minimum of 60 per cent of the total requested EU contribution should be allocated within each thematic area for that purpose. Financial support to third parties may only support results from Horizon 2020 Pillar III and Horizon Europe Pillar II projects, or owners of research results stemming from them. Proposals should demonstrate a gender-sensitive approach that addresses structural, cultural and institutional barriers faced by women innovators and entrepreneurs.

## Expected outcomes and impacts

- Owners of Horizon project results are better prepared to bring products, practices and solutions to market, with clearer value propositions, validated use cases and credible commercialisation pathways.
- Stronger links between research outcomes, Horizon Europe instruments and public and private knowledge intermediaries produce a more cohesive and responsive innovation journey.
- Enhanced collaboration across the knowledge triangle of research, education and innovation ensures more effective exploitation of impactful results and stronger alignment with societal and industrial needs.

## Conditions, eligibility and TRL

- Type of action: Research and Innovation Action; the topic sits under the Bridging actions 2027 call.
- Procedure: to ensure a balanced portfolio, grants are awarded not only in order of ranking but at least to one application highest ranked within each of areas A to E, provided all thresholds are met. A proposal in a thin area has a structural advantage.
- Legal and financial set-up: financial support to third parties permitted, capped at 150,000 euro per third party, reflecting the objective of supporting prototyping and validation or demonstration of technologies in relevant environments. Eligible costs take the form of a lump sum.
- Synergy obligation: proposals should benefit from synergies and avoid overlap with HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-03, HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-01-05 and HORIZON-CL4-2026-01-MAT-PROD-48. The two Bridging identifiers are as printed in the Work Programme, which uses an older numbering in places, so they may not match the topics as finally published.

## Fit for HWU / National Robotarium

Visibility rather than research leadership is the realistic role here. The action is designed to be led by knowledge intermediaries, which points to Heriot-Watt's research commercialisation and knowledge exchange functions as the natural bid owners, with academic input on the technical screening of exploitable robotics and AI results. Area B, clean transition in energy and transport, and Area E, raw materials and advanced materials, are where existing Heriot-Watt and National Robotarium project portfolios are richest. The listed SMEs in the Industrial and Environmental Systems group — Cyberhawk, Novosound, RoboK, Reath, Reactec, Utopi and Singul8 — are exactly the kind of entities the scouted pipeline is meant to serve, so a Heriot-Watt bid has a credible statement of demand to make.

## Points to watch

- Most of the money is cascaded to third parties, so the consortium's own research budget is small; this is a route to visibility and pipeline, not a route to funded research posts.
- The five-area allocation rule is the single most important design fact for choosing which area to target.
- The topic is one of five within a call that also covers skills bridging, climate resilience, life-science data sovereignty, Pillar II innovation advancement and research infrastructure access; the call-wide eligibility restriction on Chinese-controlled entities does not apply to this particular topic, but the restriction on the Apply AI topics in the same call does.
